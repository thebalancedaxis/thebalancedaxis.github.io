Why it matters

Understanding your metabolic health

PCOS is the most common endocrine disorder in women of reproductive age — yet it remains widely misunderstood. Here's what the science actually says.

🔬

Insulin & PCOS

Insulin resistance affects up to 80% of women with PCOS regardless of weight. Glucose spikes trigger androgen production, worsening symptoms from acne to irregular cycles.

🧬

The Genetic Layer

PCOS has a strong genetic basis. Key genes like ERBB3, DENND1A, and FSHR are implicated in follicular development, insulin signaling, and ovulatory function.

🔥

Chronic Inflammation

Low-grade systemic inflammation is a hallmark of PCOS. Inflammatory markers like NF-κB and cytokines disrupt hormonal balance and contribute to long-term metabolic risk.

⚡

Glucose & Hormones

Flattening post-meal glucose spikes — through diet, movement, and timing — can meaningfully improve LH/FSH ratios, reduce androgens, and restore cycle regularity.

🧠

The Brain–Ovary Axis

PCOS isn't just a reproductive condition. The hypothalamus–pituitary axis drives GnRH pulsatility, and disruptions here cascade into irregular ovulation and hormonal chaos.

💊

Beyond Metformin

Research into GLP-1 agonists, FSHR modulators, and drug repurposing from cancer biology is opening new, genetically-informed therapeutic avenues for PCOS.

Preprint · Under Review

Evidence-based genetic variants to gene mapping and prioritization uncovers distinct molecular pathophysiology and therapeutic landscape in PCOS patients of different ethnicities

Sindhuja Rajavelu, Debojyoti De — NIT Durgapur

An integrative population-aware computational framework maps non-coding PCOS-associated genetic variants to effector genes across East Asian and European populations. The study reveals that metabolic dysregulation dominates EAS PCOS while inflammatory and immune pathways characterize EUR PCOS — with implications for precision, population-specific therapy.
